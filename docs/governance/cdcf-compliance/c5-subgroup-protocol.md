# C5 — Impact on Vulnerable Populations

**CDCF Criterion Version:** v0.2 | Gate 1 — Required for Incubation Acceptance
**Criterion Text:** [catholicdigitalcommons.org/governance/project-governance/project-vetting-criteria#criterion-5](https://catholicdigitalcommons.org/governance/project-governance/project-vetting-criteria)

---

## Doctrinal Grounding

Pope Leo XIV identified the specific risk at the intersection of AI and vulnerable populations in his "Address to Participants in the Conference 'Artificial Intelligence and Care for Our Common Home'" (Vatican City, December 5, 2025): technology becomes a moral failure when it produces systems that allow human beings to become "merely passive consumers of content generated by artificial technology," eroding the capacity to "reflect, choose freely, love unconditionally and enter into authentic relationships." Tools that systematically degrade outcomes for vulnerable populations accelerate exactly that erosion — for the people with the least capacity to resist it.

*Antiqua et Nova* (Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith, January 28, 2025) defines algorithmic bias as "systematic and consistent errors in computer systems that may disproportionately prejudice certain groups in unintended ways." This is a magisterial definition, not a technical one. It establishes that identifying and disclosing algorithmic bias is a moral obligation under the Church's commitment to human dignity, not merely a technical best practice.

The Church's preferential option for the poor provides the evaluative standard. Catholic institutions disproportionately serve populations that commercial AI development pipelines have historically underrepresented. Deploying tools without examining their performance for these populations is inconsistent with that preferential option and is a governance failure regardless of aggregate performance metrics.

## The Specific Populations the CDCF Criterion Names

The CDCF criterion names four population categories. Any institution deploying a CDFI-scored AI tool must examine performance for the populations it actually serves within these categories.

| Population | Why They Are Specifically Named | Likely Impact on AI Doctrinal Performance |
|------------|--------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Elderly and people with disabilities | Disproportionately rely on AI for information access; lower capacity to detect errors | May use simpler, shorter prompts that activate different model behaviors |
| Recent immigrants and non-English-speaking communities | Dominant in U.S. Catholic parishes (Spanish, Portuguese, Tagalog, Vietnamese, and others); often asking AI in their native language | SAICRED v2 tested English only; no non-English performance data exists |
| Communities in poverty and rural/underserved areas | Fewer alternative sources for doctrinal information; higher dependence on AI accuracy | Lower-resource question phrasing may produce different model behaviors |
| People with heightened vulnerability to algorithmic harm | Populations underrepresented in training data | Magisterially defined in Antiqua et Nova as requiring specific examination |

## C5 Status for SAICRED v2

**Current status:** C5 subgroup evaluation has not been conducted. All 400 SAICRED v2 prompts are in English, authored by a single domain expert. No performance data exists for non-English speakers, elderly users, lower-literacy users, or any other named subgroup.

This is an honest disclosure, not a disqualifying deficiency. The CDCF criterion requires either documented analysis or explicit acknowledgment of absence paired with a concrete plan. This document provides both.

**Required disclosure language for any publication using SAICRED v2 scores:**

> *"SAICRED v2 evaluated model performance on English-language prompts authored by a single domain expert across seven Catholic doctrinal topic domains. No subgroup performance analysis across language communities, literacy levels, age groups, or populations named in the CDCF C5 criterion has been conducted. Institutions deploying AI models for use with non-English-speaking communities, elderly users, youth formation programs, people with disabilities, or communities in poverty should treat SAICRED v2 scores as English-language formation-context performance indicators and should not assume equivalent performance in those deployment contexts without additional evaluation."*

## The C5 Subgroup Evaluation Protocol

The following protocol defines what a C5-compliant evaluation requires. It is the primary deliverable for SAICRED v3. It is tradition-agnostic and transferable.

### Protocol Structure

**Four subgroup evaluation dimensions:**

| Dimension | Rationale | Method |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Language | Many U.S. Catholic parishes are majority non-English-speaking; Global South Catholic AI deployment is primarily non-English | Run the full 400-prompt dataset translated by qualified Catholic theologians into Spanish, Portuguese, and Tagalog; compare CDFI scores to English baseline |
| Reading level | Catechetical materials target a range of literacy levels | Rewrite a 50-question subset at 6th-grade reading level; test whether response quality and doctrinal accuracy degrade |
| Age/formation context | RCIA youth track and Catholic school formation involve minors with different comprehension contexts | Add a fifth prompt variant with youth-appropriate framing; test whether doctrinal accuracy is preserved |
| Question source diversity | Single-expert question authorship is a subgroup proxy; diverse question sources reduce this dependence | Add 25 questions sourced from parish formation directors in under-resourced communities; test performance stability |

**Minimum sample:** 50 questions per dimension, each in four prompt variants, across all models under evaluation.

**Performance parity threshold:** A model that clears the Formation and Catechesis threshold (CDFI >= 85) in the English baseline must score within 5 CDFI points of that threshold across all evaluated languages and question formats. A gap greater than 5 points constitutes a subgroup performance disparity requiring disclosure and re-evaluation of the tier assignment for that deployment context.

**Priority sequence for SAICRED v3:** Spanish (highest priority — largest underserved population in U.S. Catholic parishes) → Tagalog → Portuguese → reading level → youth framing → question source diversity.

## C5 Compliance Path for Adopting Institutions

An institution deploying a CDFI-scored AI tool before SAICRED v3 subgroup data is available must:

```
STEP 1 — Identify the populations your institution serves
  Map your specific community against the four CDCF categories.
  A rural parish with a predominantly elderly English-speaking
  congregation has different C5 risk exposure than an urban
  parish with a majority Spanish-speaking community.

STEP 2 — Assess your C5 gap
  For each population you serve:
    □ Does SAICRED v2 include performance data for this group?
      → If YES: cite the relevant data in your deployment decision
      → If NO: document the gap explicitly

STEP 3 — For unanalyzed populations: choose a path
  Option A: Run targeted evaluation for your population
    before deployment (use the protocol above as the template)
  Option B: Deploy with enhanced oversight and disclosure
    Use the required disclosure language above; increase
    Level 4 oversight requirements for affected contexts;
    document the decision and the gap in your governance log

STEP 4 — Document your decision
  The C5 compliance record must include:
    □ Population mapping for your institution
    □ SAICRED v2 data available for those populations
    □ Gaps identified
    □ Path chosen (evaluation or disclosed oversight)
    □ Named Level 3 authority sign-off
```

## Algorithmic Bias as a Magisterial Category

*Antiqua et Nova* defines algorithmic bias as "systematic and consistent errors in computer systems that may disproportionately prejudice certain groups in unintended ways." This definition has two implications for the CDFI Framework:

**For scoring:** The framing effect finding in SAICRED v2 — that five of six models perform 10-16 CDFI points better when the Catholic context is explicit — is a form of algorithmic bias against users who do not know to supply the Catholic context cue. A user who phrases their question neutrally receives a categorically less reliable response from most models. This bias disproportionately affects users with less familiarity with how to prompt AI systems effectively — which includes elderly users, recent immigrants, and lower-literacy users.

**For governance:** The C5 protocol above is not merely a statistical exercise. It is the institutional response to a magisterially-defined moral obligation: examining AI performance specifically for the populations most vulnerable to algorithmic harm.
